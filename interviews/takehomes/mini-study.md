# Take-home: the mini-study — the P06 format

**Navigate:** [← take-homes](README.md) · [safety researcher](../roles/safety-researcher.md)

**The prompt as issued.** "Run a small interpretability study: find
a feature, intervene on it, and report what the intervention did."
The research-family take-home — the one that most often produces an
offer, because most candidates cannot falsify.

**The mapping.** [P06](../../projects/p06-causal-interpretability-study/README.md)
— intervention, not correlation; the probe is always "how do you
know the intervention is the cause?" (controls, held-out checks).
Paper spine: scaling monosemanticity
([note 18](../../research/notes/18-towards-monosemanticity.md)),
faithfulness vs plausibility
([note 16](../../research/notes/16-faithfulness-vs-plausibility.md)).

**The rubric.**
1. **Preregistered expectation.** Written before the run: what you
   expect the intervention to do, and what result would count
   against the claim. Without this the study is a tour.
2. **Controls.** The same intervention on a feature you expect to be
   inert — the placebo discipline.
3. **Falsification attempted.** At least one attempt to break your
   own conclusion; reported either way.
4. **Provenance on every artifact.** Which model, which version,
   which access level — the register discipline in miniature.

**The honest tiers expected.** What the small scale cannot show
(single model, single family, no scaling claim); the next tier's
compute named. This format rewards the smallest honest claim that
the evidence supports — the corpus's whole method in one document.
