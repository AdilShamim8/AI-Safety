# The behavioral bank — evidence-honesty answers, not stories

**Navigate:** [← questions](README.md) · [honesty round](honesty-round.md)

**How this bank works.** Behavioral rounds fail on two patterns:
invented drama, and unmeasurable claims. This corpus trains a third
way — answer with an artifact and its boundary.

## The questions

**Q1. "Tell me about a time you were wrong."**
Use the corpus's own honesty ledger format: what was claimed, what
the evidence turned out to be, what changed. The
[honesty ledger](../../courses/honesty-ledger.md) exists because "I was wrong"
is a record-keeping discipline, not a confession.

**Q2. "Describe a hard trade-off you made."**
The abstention threshold in P03: coverage vs precision, set on a
curve with the reason stated. A trade-off you can draw is a trade-off
you made; a vibe is not.

**Q3. "Tell me about working with incomplete information."**
Every project's not-executed tiers. The answer's shape: what was run,
what was not, what the next tier would need — the exact structure of
every project report in this corpus.

**Q4. "How do you handle disagreement about technical decisions?"**
Answer with the paired-ledger pattern: both positions, both evidence,
the decision written down with its assumptions. If you have a real
example, use it; if not, say that directly — this bank rewards the
same honesty it asks about.

**Q5. "Why this field?"**
Anchor in the market layer, not in sentiment: the
[skills outlook](../market/skills-outlook.md) says 39% of core skills
change by 2030; the durable asset is the method. Your answer should
be one sentence of motivation and two sentences of evidence
discipline — interviewers in this field discount pure motivation.

**The rule.** Every behavioral answer in this bank ends with a
verifiable artifact or a plain "I don't have one." Both are respected;
fabrication never is.
