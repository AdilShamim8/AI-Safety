## 1. Identity and provenance

- **What it is:** a large corpus of *machine-generated statements* designed
  to teach and test detection of **implicit toxicity** — statements that are
  toxic by implication rather than by slur or profanity — paired with
  benign counterfactuals (same topic, toxic implication removed). Its
  generation-and-annotation loop (produce → human-annotate → use annotations
  to steer further generation) is a worked example of human–model
  collaborative dataset construction.
- **Canonical hosting inspected:** Hugging Face `skg/toxigen-data` [R1].
  Paper: arXiv:2203.09509, linked from the page [R1]. The `skg` org hosts
  the data; the research project is Microsoft-led (paper-level knowledge,
  **not re-verified this round**).
- **How WE obtained it:** page inspection only, 2026-10-21; no content
  downloaded or redistributed.

## 2. Composition (page-verified [R1])

- Overall size category on the page: **100K–1M rows** [R1] — a wide bucket;
  the card records the bucket, not a precise count, because the page did not
  display one.
- **Annotated subset: 9.9k rows** (train 8.96k, test 940) [R1].
- **Prompts split: 31k rows; annotations split: 27.5k rows** [R1].
- Task category on the page: **text classification** [R1].
- The widely-quoted "274k statements" figure for the full generation pool is
  **paper-level knowledge not verified this round** — the honest statement is
  the 100K–1M bucket plus the verified sub-splits above.

## 3. Collection and labeling

- The human annotation loop details (annotator instructions, agreement
  measures, pay): **not visible at page level**; recorded as not published
  at the achieved access level. The existence of a *separate annotated
  subset* (9.9k rows [R1]) is the page-level fact to keep: the machine-scale
  pool and the human-checked gold region are different populations, and
  conflating them is the classic error this card warns about.

## 4. Splits and leakage discipline

- Page-verified split structure [R1]: annotated train 8.96k / test 940 — a
  ~95/5 split of the annotated subset. Course discipline: any classifier
  benchmarked on the annotated test set must not have seen the 31k-row
  prompt pool if those prompts were used for generation conditioning;
  near-duplicate checking between machine-generated statements across splits
  is mandatory (machine-generated corpora are especially prone to
  paraphrase-level overlap).

## 5. Uses and known misuse

- **Measures well:** implicit-toxicity classification under a controlled
  generation process, with benign counterfactuals as hard negatives —
  the counterfactual pairing is what makes "implicit" measurable at all.
- **Known misuse patterns:** (a) quoting ToxiGen accuracy as evidence of
  general toxicity detection quality (the construct is *implicit* toxicity,
  a different and harder distribution); (b) training safety filters on the
  machine-generated pool and evaluating on human-written toxicity —
  distribution mismatch guarantees inflated numbers; (c) ignoring that
  generated statements carry generator artifacts (style, topic
  concentration) that a classifier can exploit as shortcuts.

## 6. What this dataset CANNOT support

- Human-written-toxicity claims: the statements are machine-generated by
  design (annotated subset labels are human, content is not).
- Demographic or dialect fairness claims about real users.
- Citation-quality evidence about *why* statements are toxic — the
  annotations observed at page level are category labels, not rationales.
