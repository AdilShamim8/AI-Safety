# Progress tracker — the audit-only route (reading and judging, no building)

**What this is:** the step-by-step milestone checklist for the
[audit-only route](../audit-only.md) — progress for the learner who
will not build the projects but must be able to *read, judge, and
challenge* safety claims: managers, policy staff, journalists,
procurement reviewers, and the safety-curious. Same artifact
discipline as the build routes, different artifacts: your output is
*written judgment* — reviews, challenge memos, and the questions that
catch unstated residuals. Compact version:
[careers index](../careers-index.md#progress-tracking--the-step-by-step-ladder).

**The auditor's special discipline:** you are training the skill the
field is shortest of — the ability to read a safety claim and say
*what it does not establish*. Every milestone ends in a written
challenge, not a passing grade.

- [ ] [Ch.01](../../chapters/01-risk-models-and-research-epistemics/README.md) completed (reading tier)

## M1 — Learning to read claims (~3 weeks)

- [ ] [Ch.02](../../chapters/02-evaluation-science-and-experimental-design/README.md) read; the gap sentence and construct laundering concepts internalized
- [ ] First challenge memo written: take any public safety benchmark number (a vendor blog, a model card) and write one page on what it does and does not establish
- [ ] [note 41](../../research/notes/41-model-written-evaluations.md) read: agreement numbers cannot detect omissions
- [ ] [note 49](../../research/notes/49-helm.md) read: coverage-with-named-holes as the reading standard
- [ ] The [claims register](../../research/claims-register.yaml) browsed: every claim carries its evidence strength — the habit transfers to everything else I read

**Self-audit:** What is construct laundering, with a fresh example?
Which number in my memo had no denominator? What did my chosen
benchmark NOT name in its omissions?

## M2 — The incident literacy block (~3 weeks)

- [ ] All eleven [incident reviews](../../research/notes/incidents/README.md) read — the three eras: liability/authority, rollout/channel, history
- [ ] One incident rewritten in my own words as a one-page brief for a non-technical executive, mechanism and remediation-judgment included

- [ ] [Incident 01](../../research/notes/incidents/01-air-canada-chatbot-liability.md) as the legal case: the generation channel is not a source of record

**Self-audit:** For my executive brief: did I state what the incident
does NOT demonstrate? Which era's lesson would I deploy first in a
procurement review, and why?

## M3 — The judgment instruments (~3 weeks)

- [ ] [Ch.22](../../chapters/22-safety-engineering-and-assurance/README.md) read: safety-case grammar as the reading lens
- [ ] [comparison 06](../../research/notes/comparison-06-safety-cases-vs-red-team-reports.md) read: assurance's two documents
- [ ] [Ch.23](../../chapters/23-governance-and-standards/README.md) read at survey tier
- [ ] One full safety-case critique written: take a real (or course-templated) safety case and challenge three of its claims with named evidence gaps
- [ ] The [audit-only route page](../audit-only.md)'s reading sequence completed through its checkpoint

**Self-audit:** Where does my critiqued safety case assume without
saying so? Which of its evidence is page-inspected versus hearsay —
and did the case say? What question would I put to its author that
they cannot answer from the document?

## M4 — The standing habit (~ongoing)

- [ ] A personal register started: every safety claim I encounter (news, vendor, paper) logged with source, access level, and what it establishes/does not
- [ ] The [FAQ](../../FAQ.md) read; my top three questions answered or added to my ask-list
- [ ] Ten glossary terms per week, sustained
- [ ] One colleague (or one public post) briefed per month on an incident's real mechanism — teaching as the retention test

**Self-audit:** Did my register catch me believing something at
hearsay level this week? What claim did I downgrade after checking,
and what did the source actually say?

## What this tracker does not promise

No building credentials — the audit route makes you a *better
judge*, not an engineer; the build routes remain the artifact path
to those roles. But the field's bottleneck is honest readers: the
person who can ask "what does this not establish?" in the room where
the deployment decision happens. That is the whole of this tracker's
promise, and it is enough.
