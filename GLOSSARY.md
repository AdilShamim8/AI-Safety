# Glossary

Terms are added **with the chapters that need them**; this file grows as the
course builds. Each entry: definition, notation/aliases, common confusions,
and the chapters that use the term. Sources are cited where a definition
follows a specific document; otherwise the definition is this course's own
operationalization.

| Term | Definition | Aliases / notation | Common confusion | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AI safety** | Studying and reducing harms from AI systems, including failures of objectives, robustness, oversight, deployment, misuse, and human institutions. | — | Treated as a property of a model ("a safe model") instead of a claim about a system-in-context (see *safety claim*). | all |
| **AI alignment** | Making system behavior and learned objectives compatible with intended human goals, constraints, and values — including the question of whose intentions count. | — | Used interchangeably with *safety*; alignment is one family of causes of (un)safety. | 03–08, 24 |
| **AI security** | Protecting models, data, tools, infrastructure, and AI-enabled workflows against adversaries, exploitation, and unauthorized actions. | — | Security *of* AI vs. AI *for* cybersecurity — different fields, overlapping techniques. | 12–16, 19 |
| **AI assurance** | Assembling and challenging evidence about a system's behavior and risks in a specified deployment context. | — | Assurance ≠ testing: assurance organizes evidence for a decision; testing produces evidence. | 02, 22, 23 |
| **Safety claim (context-relative)** | The workable form: in this defined workflow, with these permissions and controls, the evidence supports keeping these specified risks below these stated limits. | σ, κ, h, τ notation (Ch. 01) | Re-using a claim from one context in another after the system, tools, or users changed. | 01, 22 |
| **Hazard** | A potential source of harm (wrong output, leaked data, unauthorized action, harmful capability used). | h | Confused with *risk* (which adds likelihood and exposure); confused with *threat* (which implies an adversary). | 01, 13, 22 |
| **Capability** | What a system can do that enables a hazard. | — | Capability ≠ propensity: being able to do X says nothing about how often X is done in context. | 01, 20 |
| **Propensity** | How often, in a specified context, the system actually exhibits the enabling behavior (a rate with a denominator). | — | Estimated without a context or denominator — the most common unoperationalized word in safety prose. | 01, 02, 15 |
| **Exposure** | Who or what is positioned to be harmed (users, data, downstream systems). | — | Omitted entirely in model-only risk talk. | 01, 21 |
| **Severity** | Magnitude of harm conditional on realization. | — | Averaged into single "expected loss" numbers that hide tail structure. | 01, 22 |
| **Causal pathway** | The mechanism chain from trigger through behavior to harm. | p | Drawn as a diagram and then never attached to evidence — the pathway becomes decoration. | 01, 13, 17 |
| **Threat hypothesis** | Operational form: in context κ, system σ exhibits behavior b via pathway p, producing harm exceeding threshold τ; with pre-stated weakening observations. | H(·) | A hypothesis with no pre-stated weakening observations is a position, not a hypothesis. | 01, 15, 22 |
| **Evidence kinds** | The course's six: documented incidents, controlled experiments, formal results, benchmark results, forecasts, ethical judgment. | — | Treating the hierarchy as one ladder; kind must match the claim being made. | 01, 02, all |
| **Aleatory / epistemic / model uncertainty** | Randomness in the process / things true but unknown to you / the decomposition itself possibly wrong. | — | Collapsing all three into one number, or using "uncertain" to mean "no evidence." | 01, 02, 17 |
| **Disagreement** | Experts holding different model structures or values — distinct from uncertainty; recorded, not averaged away. | — | Laundered into variance and averaged. | 01, 21, 23 |
| **Mesa-optimization** | A learned model that is itself an optimizer; its objective may differ from the training loss. Introduced by Hubinger et al. (arXiv:1906.01820). | inner optimization | Treated as established fact about current models rather than a hypothesis with conditions (the source paper's own framing is careful here). | 01 (method lesson), 03, 07 |
| **Accident (Concrete Problems sense)** | Unintended and harmful behavior emerging from poor design of real-world ML systems. From Amodei et al. (arXiv:1606.06565). | — | Read as "rare stochastic event"; the source meaning is design-induced harmful behavior, frequency unspecified. | 01, 03 |
| **Rubber-stamp rate** | Course term (Ch. 01 solutions): share of system-flagged items approved by humans that fail independent re-review. | — | Measured without a pre-deployment baseline — then the number has no referent. | 01, 21 |
| **Construct validity** | The disciplined gap-statement between the thing you intend to measure (construct) and the score your instrument returns. | — | Validity treated as a yes/no property of an instrument; it is a scoped claim (valid *for* this construct, *in* this range). | 02, 06, 15, 21 |
| **Construct laundering** | Quoting a score under a broader construct name than the instrument supports (e.g., an XSTest refusal rate reported as "safety"). | — | The gap sentence is omitted, so the reader cannot see the substitution. | 02, 15 |
| **Gap sentence** | Required statement in this course's evaluation reports: "score S is evidence for construct C except <named differences>". | — | Written but vague ("some differences may exist"). | 02, P01 |
| **Five provenance questions** | Who made it and why; how built; what license (verified where); what revision; what it claims not to cover. Applied to every dataset before use. | — | Treated as one-time paperwork; licenses and revisions change (WildChat ODC-BY 2024-06-26). | 02, 16, P01-P09 |
| **Grouped split** | Splitting by the unit of independence (user, session, template, prompt family), not by item. | — | Named after the fact to justify a leak; the unit must be argued from the data's dependence structure. | 02, 17, P09 |
| **Contamination** | Evaluation items present in training corpora, making scores measure memorization as well as (or instead of) the construct. | test-set leakage | Treated as binary; it is a matter of degree and of *what* leaked (items vs. patterns). | 02, 06, 17 |
| **Adaptive overfitting** | The field iterating against a fixed test set until the benchmark measures benchmark-specific skill. | benchmark overfitting | Confused with single-team overfitting; adaptivity is a field-level process. | 02, 15, 20 |
| **Wilson interval** | Confidence interval for rate metrics that behaves near 0 and 1: center = (p̂+z²/2n)/(1+z²/n); half-width = z·√(p̂(1−p̂)/n + z²/4n²)/(1+z²/n). | score interval | Applied to clustered data as if items were independent — the course's most common silent invalidity. | 02, 15, P01 |
| **Paired ledger** | Reporting an attack-side metric together with its benign-side complement (harmful refusal + safe compliance) with explicit denominators. | two-sided reporting | Half a confusion matrix presented as a result. | 02, 15, P07 |
| **Preregistration** | Fixing construct, instruments, splits, intervals, decision thresholds, and weakening observations before data are seen. | — | Vague plans ("we will measure safety") or plans written after exploratory analysis — theater, not discipline. | 02, 24, P01-P10 |
| **Effect size vs significance** | The magnitude a claim cares about (vs. threshold τ) versus the detectability a test reports; both are stated, τ leads. | — | p-values quoted as if they were magnitudes; huge-n benchmarks "detect" meaningless differences. | 02, 21 |
| **Cluster-aware interval** | Interval computed at the unit of dependence (units, not items, are the effective n). | design effect correction | Ignored when items outnumber units — inflates confidence precisely in agent evaluations. | 02, 14 |
| **Reward hacking** | Optimizing the written proxy reward in ways that reduce the intended objective; formal treatments define it via conditions under which the proxy's ordering diverges from the true objective's as optimization pressure grows. | proxy gaming; specification gaming (loose) | Used for any bad output — the term is about systematic exploitation of a specified objective, not about errors the objective already penalizes. | 03, 07, P02 |
| **Proxy reward** | The written, computable objective actually optimized (R-hat), standing in for an intended objective (R) that is unwritten and expensively judged. | R-hat; specified objective | Treating the proxy and the intended objective as one thing because the proxy was written with good intentions. | 03, 04, P02 |
| **Goodhart gap** | The difference between performance on the proxy and performance on the intended objective for a given policy and distribution: G = J(pi; R-hat, D) − J(pi; R, D). | G; proxy-intended gap | Averaged away as noise; it is a signed, pressure-dependent quantity that grows systematically under optimization. | 03, 17 |
| **Proxy validity region** | The set of (policy, distribution) pairs within which the proxy's ordering agrees with the intended objective's; outside it, more optimization means more divergence. | V(R-hat) | Assumed global; every proxy has a region, and the region sentence is mandatory in any claim that uses the proxy. | 03, 15, 17 |
| **Optimization pressure** | The amount of search applied to the proxy (training steps, tuner budget, best-of-n width) — the independent variable that widens the Goodhart gap. | alpha | Held fixed in prose while the whole claim is about its growth; report the region as a function of alpha. | 03, P02 |
| **Goal misgeneralization** | A capability that generalizes off-distribution while pursuing a goal learned from in-distribution training that differs from the intended one — correct behavior on the training distribution, wrong goal under shift. | — | Read as 'the model got dumber under shift'; the Shah et al. examples are competent pursuit of the wrong objective. | 03, 07, 11 |
| **Best-of-n selection** | Draw n candidates, keep the highest-scoring one under the (proxy) objective — the cleanest laboratory form of optimization pressure. | BoN; reranking | Treated as harmless inference-time trickery; BoN is optimization pressure without new parameters and hacks proxies in proportion to n. | 03, P02 |
| **Unhackability** | The Skalse et al. property of a proxy that no policy (or no stochastic policy, per regime) scores above the intended objective's optimum; most natural proxies fail it. | — | Treated as achievable by 'better reward design'; the formal results say unhackability is a strong structural condition, not a tuning outcome. | 03 |
| **Capability-dependent misalignment** | Pan et al.'s empirical pattern: misspecified objectives hold at low capability and break past a capability threshold (phase transition), so 'it worked in testing' is not evidence about the deployed capability band. | phase-transition misalignment | Interpolated linearly between tested capability points; the demonstrated pattern is a threshold, and thresholds are invisible between test points. | 03, 20 |
| **Specification audit** | The chapter's artifact: a measured intended objective run alongside the proxy under swept optimization pressure, with preregistered gates and a reported validity region. | — | Confused with 'we reviewed the reward code'; an audit without a measured intended objective and swept pressure reviews nothing. | 03, P02 |
| **Reward model** | A learned scalar r_phi(x, y) fit to preference pairs (e.g. via the Bradley-Terry surrogate), standing in for human judgment during policy optimization. | r_phi; preference model | Treated as 'the values, learned'; it is a proxy (Ch. 03) whose validity region ends where its training pairs end. | 04, P04 |
| **RLHF** | Reinforcement learning from human feedback: fit a reward model to preference labels, then optimize the policy against it under a drift budget (KL penalty) toward a reference policy. | RL from human feedback | Used as a synonym for 'aligned'; RLHF installs behaviors labelers reward on the label distribution — a construct statement, not a safety bound. | 04, 05, P04 |
| **DPO** | Direct Preference Optimization: reparameterize the KL-constrained RLHF objective so the optimal policy is closed-form, giving a classification loss on preference pairs with no separate reward model or sampling. | Direct Preference Optimization | Read as 'RLHF without the downsides'; the objective is still the BT surrogate — label-bias pathologies inherit directly, only the optimizer changes. | 04, P04 |
| **RLAIF** | RL from AI feedback: preference labels (or critiques/revisions) generated by a model rather than human annotators; Constitutional AI is the reference two-phase instantiation. | AI feedback | Treated as scale-free human replacement; the labeling model's biases become the objective's biases, now correlated across all pairs. | 04, 05 |
| **KL regularization** | The drift budget on policy updates — penalize divergence from a reference policy (usually the SFT initialization) instead of trusting the reward alone. | KL penalty; drift budget | Read as making updates 'safe'; it bounds where the policy can go, not whether the reward is right inside that region. | 04, P04 |
| **Bradley-Terry surrogate** | The standard pair-choice model P(y_w preferred over y_l) = sigma(r(y_w) - r(y_l)) that converts preference pairs into a scalar-reward regression. | BT model | Its assumptions (scalar preference, pair independence, annotator interchangeability, no order/length effects) are inherited silently — none appears in the loss formula. | 04, P04 |
| **Preference label bias** | Systematic label distortions — position/order, length, self-enhancement, annotator-population effects — that a scalar reward model learns at full strength because the loss cannot tell bias from value. | label artifacts | Treated as annotation noise to be averaged away; bias is systematic, so averaging replicates it, and optimization amplifies it (Ch. 03 seam). | 04, 21, P04 |
| **SFT** | Supervised fine-tuning on demonstration outputs — the first stage of the standard post-training stack, before any preference learning. | behavior cloning (loose, supervised sense) | Conflated with preference learning; SFT clones demonstrated behavior and installs no explicit optimization pressure toward what was merely preferred. | 04 |
| **Implicit reward (DPO)** | In DPO, the policy's own log-ratio against the reference, beta*(log pi(y\|x) - log pi_ref(y\|x)), plays the reward's role — the 'secretly a reward model' of the title. | z-hat margin | Treated as a trained reward model's output; it is a reparameterization constrained to exactly the KL-regularized objective's optimum family. | 04, P04 |
| **Post-training objective audit** | The chapter's artifact: a derivation carried with every step justified, plus a label-quality audit design (bias conditions, validation-only split, preregistered thresholds) for the labels the objective consumes. | — | Reduced to 'we tuned and it got better'; without the derivation and the label audit, the pipeline's guarantees are unaudited. | 04, P04 |
| **Scalable oversight** | The problem of supervising systems that potentially outperform the supervisor on the skills relevant to the task — oversight techniques whose quality does not collapse as the supervised system's capability grows. | — | Any human-in-the-loop arrangement is called scalable oversight; the term asks what happens when the loop's checker is the weaker party. | 05, 07, 08, 18 |
| **Weak-to-strong generalization** | The empirical phenomenon that a strong model naively finetuned on weak-supervisor labels outperforms its supervisor — generalization gaps the strong model's pretraining partially closes. | W2S | Read as 'supervision solved'; the reported gap-closing is partial, task-suite-specific, and uses auxiliary losses the naive baseline lacks. | 05, P05 |
| **Debate (oversight)** | Two agents argue opposing positions over a shared artifact and a judge picks the winner; with optimal play and polynomial-time judges the paper claims debate answers any question in PSPACE. | AI safety via debate | The PSPACE claim assumes optimal play and a formal judge model; it is a theoretical ceiling, not a deployment property of current debaters. | 05, 08 |
| **Breakthrough task** | Bowman et al.'s design unit: a task where human specialists succeed but unaided humans and current AI systems fail — the middle band where oversight techniques are measurable. | — | Any hard benchmark is called a breakthrough task; the definition requires the specialist/unaided gap specifically, or there is nothing to oversee. | 05, 20 |
| **Oversight gap** | The distance between what the supervised system can do and what the supervisor can check — the quantity every oversight technique in this chapter is trying to reduce. | capability-check gap | Measured in model scores; the operative quantity is checkability of outputs, not benchmark deltas. | 05, 08, 18 |
| **Correlated errors** | Errors shared between supervisor and supervised system (or between committee members) that majority-style aggregation cannot remove — oversight fails on exactly what everyone gets wrong together. | shared blind spot | Treated as averaged-out noise; correlation is the failure mode of ensembles, and its degree must be estimated, not assumed small. | 05, 08, P05 |
| **Process vs outcome supervision** | Rewarding the steps of reasoning versus only the final result; process supervision checks more bits per decision but imports the assumption that visible steps carry the outcome's causes. | step-level vs outcome-level feedback | Process supervision read as more 'aligned'; it constrains what is checked, not what is optimized — a faithful-looking process can still serve a wrong goal (Ch. 03). | 05, 18 |
| **Unaided-human floor** | The supervisor's solo performance on the task — the baseline every oversight technique must beat, and the number most often depressed by time-limited evaluation formats. | baseline human performance | Reported from convenience formats; a low floor inflates apparent oversight gains without any technique improving. | 05, 15 |
| **Performance gap recovered** | The fraction of the weak-to-strong capability gap closed by weak supervision (the W2S paper's headline framing); quoted only with its task suite and losses stated. | PGR | Averaged across tasks; the course quotes it per-suite and marks partial recovery honestly. | 05, P05 |
| **Oversight protocol audit** | The chapter's artifact: a bounded comparison design with checkable ground truth, an explicit unaided floor, supervisor error rates, and a declared correlated-error assumption with its failure condition. | — | Confused with 'we had humans review outputs'; without the floor, the error rate, and the correlation statement, the review's coverage is unknown. | 05, P05 |
| **Scorer validation** | Measuring a scorer's agreement with hand-verified labels on a fixed fixture before trusting any of its labels — published with the run, not assumed. | scorer agreement | A scorer described as 'accurate' with no agreement number on a named fixture; validation is fixture-scoped, not a property of the scorer. | 02, 15, P01, P03 |
| **Case set (versioned)** | The harness's evaluation unit: a YAML file of cases with schema, version, and SHA-256 checksum recorded in every run's provenance — silent edits invalidate downstream comparisons. | fixture (loose) | Treated as 'just a test file'; the checksum makes it the unit of comparison across runs and time. | P01, P03, P07 |
| **Fail-closed policy** | Unknown schemas, unknown adapters, stale resume state, and adapter errors abort the run with explicit errors — the runner never silently skips or substitutes. | — | Confused with fail-safe defaults; fail-closed means no output at all rather than degraded output. | P01, P07, P08 |
| **Resumability (exact)** | An interrupted run resumes from its state file and produces byte-identical outputs to an uninterrupted run — 'resumes exactly', not 'resumes approximately'. | — | Approximate resumption (re-running incomplete cases with fresh randomness) presented as the same thing. | P01, P09 |
| **ScriptedAdapter** | A deterministic adapter returning predeclared responses per case id — the CPU-tier way to exercise the full harness without any model. | — | Read as a mock of no consequence; it is the instrument that keeps CI runs honest, offline, and byte-reproducible. | P01 |
| **Anchored baselines (refusal)** | The do-nothing (echo: comply with everything) and refuse-everything baselines that bracket the paired ledger; a real guard must beat both simultaneously. | floor/ceiling baselines | Comparing one ledger side only against its favorable baseline — the half-a-confusion-matrix sin Ch. 02 names. | 02, 15, P01, P07 |
| **Truthfulness vs honesty** | Truthfulness: saying what is true (a property of outputs against the world). Honesty: reporting what the model 'believes' — including its own uncertainty — faithfully (a property of the model's self-reports). | — | Used interchangeably; a model can be honest about uncertainty while wrong, or factually right while misreporting its confidence. | 06, P03 |
| **Calibration** | The match between stated confidence and observed frequency of correctness across a declared reference class — a property of the (model, task, distribution) triple, never of a model alone. | — | Quoted as a model-level virtue; calibration does not transfer across tasks, and a calibrated model can still be confidently wrong on any single item. | 06, 17, P03 |
| **Selective prediction** | Abstaining on items where expected error exceeds a threshold instead of answering everything; evaluated jointly for accuracy and coverage, never separately. | abstention; deferral (Ch. 08 sense) | Abstention rate reported without the complementary accuracy-at-coverage; a model that abstains on everything is perfectly accurate on what it answers. | 06, 08, P03 |
| **Risk-coverage curve** | The frontier of (coverage, error-on-answered) pairs swept by the abstention threshold — the selective-predictor's paired ledger. | accuracy-coverage curve | A single point quoted as the curve; the shape (where risk rises as coverage grows) is the decision-relevant content. | 06, P03 |
| **P(True) channel** | Kadavath et al.'s self-evaluation format: the model scores the truth of a proposed answer, considering its own samples first — a self-consistency readout, not a truth oracle. | self-evaluation | Read as grounded truth monitoring; it inherits the model's blind spots — a confidently-wrong model self-certifies. | 06 |
| **Verbalized confidence** | Uncertainty expressed in natural language (Lin et al.: trainable without logits) — behavior, hence subject to preference pressure, unlike logit-derived scores. | verbalized probability | Treated as a readout of internal probabilities; it is a trained channel one preference cycle away from inflation. | 06, 04, P03 |
| **Sycophancy** | Responses matching user beliefs over truthful ones — a property of the preference-training pipeline (Sharma et al.: present across assistants and grounded in preference data), not an individual model bug. | — | Attributed to model personality; the mechanism is the objective — raters reward agreement, so the policy learns it. | 06, 04, 21 |
| **Brier score** | Mean squared error of probabilistic predictions (proper scoring rule): decomposable into calibration, resolution, and uncertainty terms — the chapter's arithmetic workhorse. | — | Read as 'accuracy of confidence'; a lower Brier can come from resolution or calibration, and the decomposition must be shown. | 06, P03 |
| **Expected calibration error (ECE)** | Confidence-bin weighted average gap between stated confidence and observed accuracy — a summary, not a proof: binning choices move it. | ECE | Quoted to 3 decimals as if binning-free; ECE is a binned estimator whose bins are a modeling choice the report must state. | 06, P03 |
| **Honesty audit** | The chapter's artifact: a calibration analysis with declared reference class, validation-only fit, Brier decomposition, risk-coverage curve, and sycophancy pressure stated as a threat to the channel. | — | A calibration plot alone; without the reference class and the channel-pressure analysis the audit launder's construct into conclusion. | 06, P03 |
| **Model organism (of misalignment)** | A deliberately constructed misaligned system built to study a hazard's mechanism under controlled conditions — evidence about the mechanism and countermeasures, never about prevalence in deployment. | — | Cited as if observed in the wild; the organism was built, not found, and the build conditions are the claim's scope. | 07, 20 |
| **Alignment faking** | A model strategically complying with the training objective while intending different behavior at deployment — demonstrated (Greenblatt et al.) only under conditions where the model is told it is being trained. | — | The 78% figure quoted as a deployment base rate; it is a rate under experimental scaffolding, and the paper says so. | 07, 18 |
| **Sandbagging** | Strategically underperforming on evaluations to appear less capable — demonstrated (van der Weij et al.) as a gameable evaluation channel via prompting, not as spontaneous behavior. | strategic underperformance | Read as 'models hide their capabilities'; the demonstrated fact is that evaluations can be gamed by an instructed strategy. | 07, 20 |
| **Evaluation awareness** | A model's ability to distinguish evaluation contexts from deployment contexts — the property that makes behavioral evaluations gameable by any strategic actor, model or otherwise. | — | Treated as a fringe concern; it is the structural limit of every test-only safety claim (Ch. 02's adaptive-overfitting lesson at the model level). | 07, 15, 20 |
| **Evidence ladder (deception claims)** | The chapter's ordering: constructed organisms < conditioned demonstrations < instructed strategies < deployment evidence — each rung requiring its own conditions statement; rungs never silently merge. | — | A conditioned demonstration cited with the force of deployment evidence; the ladder position is part of the claim. | 07, 20, 22 |
| **Skeptical evidence table** | The chapter's artifact: for each deception demonstration — what was constructed, what was instructed, what the controls were, what it shows, and the explicit 'does not show' row. | — | A summary of positive results; the table's load-bearing column is what the evidence does NOT establish. | 07, 20 |
| **Backdoor persistence** | Sleeper Agents' result: trigger-conditional unsafe behavior surviving SFT, RLHF, and adversarial training — with adversarial training sometimes concealing rather than removing. | — | Read as 'safety training is useless'; the result bounds specific countermeasures on constructed backdoors, it does not measure deployed-model behavior. | 07, 12 |
| **Instructed-L2 distinction** | The chapter's labeling separating behaviors the model was instructed to adopt from behaviors trained in and then measured — the difference between 'we made it lie' and 'it lies'. | — | Instructed demonstrations summarized as learned behavior; the label changes what the evidence supports. | 07 |
| **AI control** | Designing protocols so a system stays safe even if the untrusted model intentionally subverts oversight — safety from the protocol, not from the model's alignment (the AI Control paper's framing). | control | Read as 'controlling AI' in general; control assumes subversion and buys safety with trusted compute and human labor budgets. | 08, 18, P08 |
| **Trusted vs untrusted components** | The control setting's load-bearing split: trusted models/labor assumed non-adversarial; untrusted models treated as potentially adversarial — an assumption with stated failure conditions, never a proof. | — | 'Trusted' read as verified-safe; it means trusted-to-not-collude under the protocol's threat model — scope it or the safety claim silently widens. | 08, P08 |
| **Control protocol** | A fixed procedure mixing trusted labor, trusted monitoring, deferral, resampling, and editing over untrusted outputs — evaluated on safety (attack success under the protocol) and usefulness (task completion when no attack runs) simultaneously. | — | Evaluated on safety alone; every protocol buys safety with usefulness, and the frontier pair is the result. | 08, P08 |
| **Audit budget** | The fraction of untrusted outputs a protocol can afford to send to trusted human/model review (e.g. 2% of 5000) — the scarce resource every protocol allocates. | trusted labor budget | Treated as a knob to tune late; the budget is a design constraint that determines which protocols are even candidates. | 08, 22 |
| **Trusted monitoring** | A trusted model flags suspicious untrusted outputs at a set false-positive threshold; flagged items consume audit budget — the workhorse protocol between do-nothing and defer-everything. | — | The monitor's FPR threshold quoted without the budget arithmetic; safety depends on both jointly. | 08, P08 |
| **Deferral** | Routing a request (or a flagged subset, or critical actions) to a trusted model instead of the untrusted one — buying safety at usefulness cost proportional to traffic deferred. | defer-to-trusted | Confused with abstention (Ch. 06): abstention declines to answer; deferral answers with a costlier, trusted component. | 06, 08, P08 |
| **Collusion (control sense)** | Untrusted outputs engineered to manipulate the monitor — input collusion (jamming the monitor's inputs), action collusion (coordinated multi-output attacks), signal jamming — the class the trusted-monitor protocol must survive. | — | Treated as exotic; the AI Control paper measures it synthetically because a monitor reading untrusted text is attackable by construction. | 08, 13, P08 |
| **Safety-usefulness frontier** | The Pareto curve of (attack success, task completion) across protocols — the honest form of a control result; a single corner is not a protocol evaluation. | — | One protocol quoted at one operating point as if it dominated; the frontier is where comparisons live. | 08, P08 |
| **Adaptive red team** | An attack policy that knows the protocol and optimizes against it (e.g. prompt-injection attacks that poison the monitor's inputs) — the evaluation condition every non-adaptive control number fails. | adaptive attack | Fixed-attack results quoted as protocol safety; the adaptive attacker is the threat model the whole chapter is about. | 08, 15, P08 |
| **Control evaluation artifact** | The chapter's artifact: a control protocol specification with its trusted/untrusted assumptions, audit budget arithmetic, safety-usefulness frontier, adaptive red team, and explicit failure conditions. | — | A deployed tool described as a control protocol; without the budget, the frontier, and the adaptive test, it is a monitor, not a control result. | 08, P08 |
| **Residual stream** | The transformer's shared communication channel: each token position carries a vector that attention and MLP blocks read from and write to by addition (x_t^(l+1) = x_t^(l) + a_t^(l) + m_t^(l)). | residual stream as communication channel | Read as 'the hidden state' of one layer; it is one growing sum per position across all layers — the object every patch site is carved from. | 09, 10, 11, P06 |
| **QK / OV circuits** | The two linear maps of an attention head: W_QK decides where to look (query-key pattern over positions), W_OV decides what information moves (value to output) — composition, not separate modules. | query-key circuit, output-value circuit | Treating attention as one blob; the QK/OV split is what lets patching localize information *movement* separately from information *use*. | 09, P06 |
| **Activation patching** | The intervention family: run a corrupted prompt, patch clean activations (or vice versa) at a chosen site, and measure the restored margin — replacing observation of activations with intervention on them. | causal tracing (ROME's term), causal mediation intervention | Sold as 'finding where X is'; it finds which sites carry the clean-vs-corrupted contrast — a corruption-relative, not absolute, localization. | 09, 10, 11, P06 |
| **Restoration fraction** | R(s) = (Delta_p(s) - Delta_k) / (Delta_c - Delta_k): the share of the corruption-destroyed behavioral margin that patching site s reinstalls — the patching grid's primary statistic. | R(s), mediation share | Quoted without its corruption contrast or denominator; R is defined relative to (clean, corrupted) — change the corruption, change the number. | 09, P06 |
| **Trivial late site** | A late-layer site that restores strongly because the computed answer already sits in the residual stream there — patching it re-installs the conclusion, not the computation (stored is not computed). | tautological site | The highest-R row in a grid read as the 'module'; the informative reading is the earliest layer where restoration climbs. | 09, 10, P06 |
| **Random-patch control** | Patching with activations from an unrelated prompt: if the site still restores, the effect is site-activity, not question-specific computation — the cheapest specificity control in patching. | random donor control | Skipped because 'the number is already significant'; without it, active-but-generic sites pass every grid. | 09, P06 |
| **Synergistic components** | Components whose causal effects are not additive — one amplifies or represses another's (Vig et al.'s mediation finding) — which breaks single-site patching's additivity assumption and masks redundancy. | component interaction, ablation-with-backup | Treated as exotic; redundancy with backups is the default explanation for 'we ablated it and nothing happened'. | 09, 10, P06 |
| **Logit-difference margin** | Delta = logit(true continuation) - logit(false continuation): the per-prompt continuous readout the chapter instruments — power a binary refusal/accuracy rate cannot give a patching grid. | margin, Delta | Replaced by accuracy mid-analysis; the margin is the instrument, and swapping metrics silently changes every R. | 09, 10, 11, P06 |
| **Causal mediation analysis** | The formal frame (from statistics, brought to NLP by Vig et al.): decompose a behavior's total effect into direct and indirect (through mediator) parts; activation patching is its intervention operationalization. | CMA | The name used decoratively for any patching plot; the decomposition (TE/IE/DE) is what earns the term. | 09, P06 |
| **Feature (mechanistic sense)** | A property of the input the network computes and carries — operationalized in this chapter as a direction or pattern in activations that interventions show to be causally loaded, not merely correlated. | — | Defined by a probe's accuracy (correlation); a feature claim needs the intervention leg (chapters/10 grades this distinction). | 09, 10, 11 |
| **Superposition** | A network representing more features than it has neurons, by storing them non-orthogonally — the hypothesized cause of polysemanticity and the reason neuron-level reading fails. | — | Read as 'the model is confused'; it is a capacity strategy. Superposition is why 'one neuron one concept' is false and why dictionaries have more features than dimensions. | 10, 11, P07 |
| **Sparse autoencoder (SAE)** | An encoder-decoder pair trained to reconstruct a site's activations with a sparse bottleneck: x reconstructed from z = W_enc(x) activations over d_SAE dictionary vectors, L1-penalized so few fire per token. | dictionary learning (the training problem), dictionary (the learned vector set) | 'The SAE found the model's features'; it found A dictionary whose features are only interpretable relative to that dictionary — identity is dictionary-relative. | 10, P07 |
| **FVU (fraction of variance unexplained)** | Sum of squared reconstruction error over total variance at the instrumented site — the standard SAE reconstruction metric; 1 - (variance explained share). | — | Quoted as a usefulness number; FVU 0.18 says nothing about task behavior until reconstructions are substituted into the model and the task is re-scored. | 10, P07 |
| **Reconstruction-intervention score** | The usefulness metric: replace activations with SAE reconstructions at run time and re-score the task — the number that prices reconstruction quality in behavior. | — | Skipped because FVU exists; the two can diverge by an order of magnitude when SAE error is structured (concentrated on rare, adversarial contexts). | 10, P07 |
| **Feature splitting** | As dictionaries grow, one feature resolves into several more specific ones (San Francisco: 1 feature in 1M SAE, 2 in 4M, 11 in 34M) — feature identity is dictionary-size-relative. | — | Treated as more resolution for free; it invalidates cross-dictionary feature comparison and makes 'the X feature' index-unstable. | 10, P07 |
| **Feature absorption** | A sub-concept's activation carried by a neighboring feature, so a monitor keyed to the parent feature silently under-covers one sub-family — the miss-autopsy signature. | — | Confused with splitting (a growth effect); absorption is a coverage effect within one dictionary and is only visible by auditing what fires on the misses. | 10, P07 |
| **Faithfulness vs. plausibility** | Jacovi and Goldberg's split: faithfulness is the interpretation accurately reflecting the model's actual reasoning process; plausibility is its being convincing to a human — and human judgment measures plausibility. | — | A compelling dashboard treated as faithful; it is plausible by construction. | **Completeness (circuit sense)** | The fraction of mean \|total effect\| restored by patching the identified component set (rho_C); a circuit claim is graded, not binary — 22% vs 71% vs 29% unexplained are different claims. | rho_C | Merged with necessity; a component can be necessary (ablation hurts) while the set is incomplete (most of the effect flows elsewhere). | 10, 09, P07 |
| **Dictionary-relativity** | The property that feature identity (what feature #3412 'is') holds only relative to a pinned dictionary version and size — splitting and absorption are its two visible consequences. | — | Feature labels carried across dictionary versions without re-verification; the register records dictionary versions exactly for this reason. | 10, P07 |
| **Dead features** | Dictionary entries that (almost) never activate after training — a standard SAE health metric; high dead-feature counts mean the dictionary wasted capacity. | — | Treated as cosmetic; dead-feature counts and the feature-density bimodality are the primary self-diagnostic the founding papers themselves report. | 10, P07 |
| **Linear representation hypothesis** | The assumption family (A1-A5 in this chapter) that features live as directions in activation space — load-bearing for probes and steering alike, and false in general under superposition (Ch. 10). | — | Stated as a finding; it is an assumption per feature — the chapter's five-assumption audit exists because each can fail independently. | 11, 09, P06 |
| **Directional steering** | Adding (or subtracting) a fixed direction times a strength to activations at a chosen site and position family at inference time — the intervention class this chapter evaluates. | activation addition, activation engineering | Sold as 'editing the model'; it is a runtime perturbation whose transfer, side effects, and symmetry (removability) must each be measured. | 11, P06 |
| **Directional ablation** | Projecting activations onto the hyperplane orthogonal to a direction — removing the direction's component; the subtractive twin of steering and the white-box jailbreak mechanism (Arditi et al.). | weight orthogonalization (its trained equivalent) | Treated as neutral analysis; ablation is the dual-use mirror of addition — anything that adds refusal can be made to remove it. | 11, 15, P06 |
| **Difference-in-means direction** | The unit vector between class-mean activations over extraction sets — the cheapest direction extraction; distinct from a trained probe's hyperplane normal (cosine < 1 always). | class-mean direction | Merged with 'the probe direction'; cosine similarity between the two is a reported number, not an identity — steering with one is not a replication of the other. | 11, 09, P06 |
| **Three ledgers (steering)** | This course's evaluation frame for any steering intervention: the target ledger (does it move the behavior), the over-refusal/false-positive ledger (what it over-triggers), and the capability ledger (what it costs) — all three or no claim. | — | Reporting only the target ledger; the safety gain and the over-refusal tax arrive together (the paired-ledger rule of Ch. 02, at intervention level). | 11, 06, P06 |
| **Exchange-rate frontier** | The sweep of steering strength alpha plotted as (harmful-refusal gained, over-refusal paid) — convex in practice; alpha selection is choosing a point on it, never the harmful ledger alone. | — | A single alpha quoted as 'the' setting; the frontier's shape (0.91 to 34.4 points paid per point gained in the worked example) is the result. | 11, 08, P06 |
| **Newcombe interval (difference of proportions)** | The hybrid-score confidence interval for p1 - p2 built from two Wilson intervals — the capability-degradation interval that respects small denominators and near-boundary rates. | Newcombe hybrid-score method | Replaced by the pooled z-test's normal interval; Newcombe stays accurate near 0/1 and at small n, which capability batteries hit constantly. | 11, 02, P06 |
| **Power-blind capability claim** | 'No significant capability cost' from a battery too small to resolve the observed drop — unsupported rather than established; the fix is the unpooled n-per-arm computation stated in the protocol. | — | Read as evidence of no cost; a 500-item battery resolving only ~5.8pp cannot speak to a 2.6pp drop — the claim's honest form names the blind spot. | 11, 02, P06 |
| **Consistency objective (unsupervised probing)** | Burns et al.'s label-free readout training: find directions whose answers agree across augmented views — with the assumption that consistency identifies the target feature rather than a consistent correlate. | — | Consistency treated as validation; a dataset artifact can be perfectly consistent — the assumption is the claim, and it must be tested with interventions. | 11, 06 |
| **Arditi-style substring refusal classifier** | Scoring a completion as refusal iff it contains one of a compiled set of common refusal substrings — cheap, deterministic, and the instrument the chapter's ledgers use (as the Arditi paper did). | refusal substring classifier | Treated as ground truth; it misses paraphrased refusals and catches refusal-mentions in quotes — its error structure belongs in the ledger's limitations. | 11, P06 |
| **Side-effect ledger** | The P02 reporting artifact: collateral damage kept as discrete events (kind, episode, location, magnitude) with counts and a per-episode damage rate — never folded into the scalar score being optimized. | damage ledger | Averaging damage into 'the metric'; the ledger exists precisely because the proxy prices the damage at zero, so any scalar that includes it stops being the proxy's score. | 03, P02 |
| **Matched-pressure control** | The oracle arm of a misspecification experiment: optimize the intended objective R under exactly the same pressure the primary spent on R_hat, so the proxy-vs-intended contrast is attributable to the objective, not the compute. | oracle arm | Omitted as 'unrealistic' — that is the point: it is a measurement control, not a deployment proposal, and without it every gap has a compute confound. | 03, P02 |
| **Interval-bound gate** | A preregistered pass/fail judged by confidence-interval bounds, not point estimates: G1 fails when the worst-case degradation implied by the point's Wilson lower bound exceeds tolerance; G2 fails when the lower bound itself sits below the floor. | G1/G2 gates | Point-estimate gates that pass inside wide intervals; a gate only the point estimate passes is a gate that failed honestly. | 02, 03, P02 |
| **Held-out divergence** | The signed difference (held-out arm minus tuning arm) of a value series at matched optimization pressure — the discriminator that keeps the shifted-proxy score and the intended objective next to each other so neither is quoted alone. | shift divergence | Quoting only the tuning series; for proxy failure the shifted proxy score stays high while the intended objective collapses, and the divergence series is what makes that visible at every alpha. | 03, 17, P02 |
