# ISO/IEC 42001: AI management systems

**ISO/IEC 42001:2023** (published December 2023) is the certifiable AI
management-system standard — "ISO 2701 for AI." If your organization
wants *independent, auditable* proof of AI governance (often for
enterprise sales, insurance, or regulator relations), this is the
document your management system gets certified against.

## What it actually requires

A **management system** (MS) in the ISO sense: a repeatable PDCA
(plan-do-check-act) loop with documented controls, owned by named roles,
audited on a schedule. ISO 42001 structures it as:

- **Context & leadership**: AI policy, roles & responsibilities, an AI
  governance committee with actual authority.
- **Planning**: AI risk assessment (cross-referencing **ISO/IEC 23894:2023**,
  the AI risk-management guidance), objectives, and an *AI system
  inventory* — every system, purpose, lifecycle stage, and owner.
- **Support & operation**: competence records, data management (quality,
  provenance, consent), documented development & change processes.
- **Performance evaluation**: monitoring, internal audit, management
  review — with defined metrics per system.
- **Improvement**: incident processes, nonconformity handling,
  continual-improvement records.

Plus **Annex D controls** (38 controls in 10 themes, from AI development
practices to documentation and bias mitigation) mapped from the
ISO 27001-style control structure.

## Why engineers should care

1. **It harmonizes the alphabet soup.** ISO 42001's inventory-and-controls
   structure is designed to absorb NIST AI RMF artifacts (MAP/MEASURE
   outputs become planning/evaluation evidence) and produce EU AI Act
   conformity documentation from the same records. One system, three
   regimes.
2. **It forces an inventory.** The single highest-value artifact in
   multi-team AI governance is "every AI system we run, with owner and
   risk status" — 42001 makes it mandatory, which is why it exists in
   exactly zero unregulated organizations otherwise.
3. **Certification is a market passport.** Large EU and US enterprise
   procurement 2025+ increasingly asks for AI governance proof; 42001
   certification is the cleanest answer currently on offer.

## The certification path (realistic version)

1. Gap assessment against Annex D controls (typically 2–4 weeks with a
   consultant).
2. Stand up the MS: policy, inventory, risk register (reuse NIST-RMF
   mappings), incident process, competence records.
3. Run the loop once: internal audit → management review → corrective
   actions. (Auditors require evidence of *operation*, not just
   documents.)
4. Stage 1 (documentation) and Stage 2 (implementation) external audits
   by an accredited certification body; surveillance annually.

Typical timeline for a focused org: 4–9 months to first certification.

## Relationship to this repo

The repository supplies the *MEASURE-side* machinery an ISO 42001 / NIST
RMF / EU AI Act evidence base needs:

- benchmark harnesses producing versioned eval records
  (`scripts/run_benchmark.py`),
- incident-style case documentation patterns (`docs/incidents/`),
- documented risk taxonomy pages (`docs/risks/`) usable as the risk
  register's background material, and
- CI-enforced change management (`.github/workflows/`).

Governance frameworks ask "prove it"; this repo's position is that the
proving layer should be code.
