# 3.05 — Aligning with organizational goals

*Course companion note · [AI Security and Risk Management](README.md) ·
lesson 5 of chapter 3 (video, 50 XP) · [course page](https://app.datacamp.com/learn/courses/ai-security-and-risk-management)*

## Where this lesson sits

The chapter's second video: security goes beyond protecting systems — it supports broader goals including ethical and social responsibility. Security decisions ripple into customer trust, brand reputation, and legal standing.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. Different strategies, different measures

Integrating security activities with business values — responsible AI development, ethical principles, corporate social responsibility — creates a solid foundation. Market growth may dictate one approach; a conservative strategy is essential where privacy and trust are part of the business model.

### 2. Two sides to every decision

The video's worked example: advanced encryption for data protection creates strong safeguards, supporting a goal of market leadership in customer trust — yet the same decision can limit the agility of AI systems and slow innovation. Every security choice carries both faces, and the point is to SEE both before choosing.

### 3. The start-up versus the bank, in depth

The start-up: innovation is the lifeblood and competitive edge, so security measures support rapid development and deployment — agile threat modeling for emerging technologies, emphasizing speed and adaptability, open-source tools for flexibility, accepting higher risks; security practices help rather than hinder innovation, reflecting the focus on breaking new ground and capturing market opportunities. The bank: security and reliability above all — sensitive financial data and the regulatory landscape demand strict protocols; comprehensive threat modeling to reduce data integrity and privacy risks; objectives of trust, customer loyalty, and regulatory compliance demand a cautious approach to innovation.

### 4. The link, stated as a rule

The start-up sees AI security as ENABLING innovation, willing to navigate higher risks for speed and market capture; the bank sees it as a critical FOUNDATION of trust and regulatory compliance, prioritizing robustness and reliability even if innovation slows. The rule: start from a clear understanding of the goals (innovation? speed to market? customer trust? compliance?), let security strategy support those objectives by balancing risk, innovation, and responsibility — and never treat security as a stand-alone item; it is an integral part of how the organization does business.

## Worked example (beginner walk-through)

One decision, both faces. The measure: advanced encryption on customer data. *Face one:* it creates strong safeguards for customer information, supporting a goal of market leadership in customer trust — and it satisfies regulators. *Face two:* the same decision can limit the agility of the AI systems and slow innovation — the agility the strategy also wanted. The video's rule: see both faces BEFORE choosing, because the choice is not "encrypt or not" but "which goal buys the other." The start-up reads the pair one way (open-source tools, flexible, higher accepted risk, security helping rather than hindering innovation); the bank reads it the other way (strict protocols, comprehensive threat modeling, cautious innovation, trust and compliance above speed). Neither reading is wrong; the failure is choosing without seeing the second face.
