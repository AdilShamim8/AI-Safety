# The two registers — technical depth vs. accessible breadth

*Part of the [lecture library](../README.md) · 2026-10-27*

The library splits into two registers, and the split teaches
something about the field itself.

**The technical register** (Harvard, DeepMind) assumes you can read
a loss curve and treats the audience as future builders. Its
currency is mechanisms — SAEs, control protocols, RLVR — and its
honesty instruments are evaluation science and reading primary
sources. Its blind spots: the deployment context, the law, the
public — everything that is not the model.

**The accessible register** (CAIS, Safeshield) assumes the audience
will make or receive decisions about systems they cannot build. Its
currency is taxonomies, obligations, and process — risk families,
principles, audit trails. Its honesty instruments are attribution
and documentation. Its blind spots: the mechanism layer — why the
model actually misbehaves, which defenses can work in principle.

**Why this repository pairs both.** The repo's own spine is
technical-register (24 chapters of mechanisms with the math on),
but its final arc (Ch. 21–23) is deliberately accessible-register.
A learner who only speaks one register will misprice real risks:
technical-only learners propose mechanisms that institutions cannot
adopt; accessible-only learners demand assurances mechanisms cannot
provide. The lecture library's watch guide schedules both registers
against the same chapters for exactly this reason.
