# What each course does that this repository does not

*Part of the [lecture library](../README.md) · 2026-10-27*

The honest complement map — what a learner gets from each source
that the 24 chapters alone do not supply:

- **From Harvard CS 2881:** live contact with working researchers
  (the guest roster is a who's-who of the evaluation, security, and
  interpretability programs); student experiments as a model of
  bounded empirical work; the reading lists as a curated, current
  map of the primary literature; and the seminar format itself —
  defending work aloud.
- **From the DeepMind course:** a frontier lab's own prioritization,
  stated publicly and compressed — a document no third party could
  write; the exercises as a checkpoint design worth copying; and
  the honest experience of being taught by the entity being
  regulated.
- **From the CAIS course:** the collective-action chapter the
  technical curriculum leaves out; a published textbook's stability
  (fixed, citable, review-backed); and the register in which most
  real-world AI decisions are actually discussed.
- **From the Safeshield series:** the Monday-morning layer — what an
  auditor asks, what a policy hierarchy looks like, what a log must
  contain; the frameworks taught as implementable practice rather
  than as concepts; and the professional vocabulary of compliance.

The repository returns the favor each course cannot: a method that
converts all four into checkable claims — the registers, the
exercises, the honest access levels, and the chapter spine that
makes the disagreement between any two sources a findable,
priceable object.
