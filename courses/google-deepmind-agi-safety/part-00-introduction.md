## What this part covers

The course's front door (3 minutes): what the series
is, who it is for, and how it is organized — a frontier lab's safety
team explaining its own view of the alignment problem to a general
technical audience, in three parts (risks; alignment approaches;
institutions) with embedded exercises.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. A lab's self-curriculum as a primary source

The introduction's real content is its existence: Google DeepMind's
safety research group judged that the most useful thing it could
publish for the field's onboarding was not a paper but a course —
accessible, exercise-checked, and opinionated in its selection. Read
as a document, the selection *is* the message: the topics chosen
(instrumental subgoals, amplified oversight, control, dangerous-capability
evaluations) are the ones the lab's own risk model treats as
load-bearing, and the topics absent (sycophancy detail, agent-security
specifics) are equally informative.

### 2. The three-part promise

The structure — (1) why superhuman-capable systems could pursue
misaligned goals, (2) the research program that might prevent that,
(3) the institutional layer that wraps both — is itself a claim: that
alignment decomposes into technical mechanisms *plus* institutions,
and that neither alone suffices. The repo's structure (technical
chapters 01–18, sociotechnical/governance chapters 19–24) makes the
same bet.

### 3. How to take a compressed course

At 3–9 minutes per video, the format demands one load-bearing idea
per part and no filler — which makes it an excellent *pre-study*
pass before this repository's chapters: vocabulary and map first,
depth second. The library's watch guide (see
[courses/README.md](../README.md)) schedules exactly that.
