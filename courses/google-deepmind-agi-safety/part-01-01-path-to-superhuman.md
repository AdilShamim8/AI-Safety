# Part 1.1 — We are on a path to superhuman capabilities

*Course companion note · [Google DeepMind AGI Safety Course](README.md) ·

## What this part covers

The risk model's foundation stone (4 minutes): the
claim that current trajectories — scaling, RL on verifiable domains,
agentic scaffolding — lead toward systems with superhuman performance
on at least some economically and strategically significant tasks.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. What "the path" claims, precisely

The careful version of the claim is not "AGI by 20XX" but: the
capability trend line (METR-style task-horizon doubling, benchmark
saturation, agentic-tooling gains) does not flatten on its own, and
no known barrier stops it before human-level on substantial task
families. Safety work is therefore scheduled against a *trend*, not a
date — the exact posture this repo's
[Ch. 01](../../chapters/01-risk-models-and-research-epistemics/README.md)
teaches: price trajectories as conditional claims with leading
indicators, not predictions.

### 2. Why a lab says this about its own products

A frontier lab teaching capability escalation as the *first* safety
fact is notable: the risk model begins from the thing the lab is
building. That framing does two things — it makes the safety program
legible as *about the actual roadmap* (not a hypothetical), and it
publicly commits the lab to caring about the trajectory it just
described. Both are useful for outside evaluators to hold the lab to.

### 3. Superhuman *where* matters

The claim is domain-indexed: superhuman at protein design, cyber
offense, or ML research itself is a different risk object than
superhuman at chess. The strategic subset — tasks whose mastery
accelerates capability growth or bypasses oversight — is what Part
1.2's "deliberate planning" worry attaches to. Chapter 20's
dangerous-capability evaluation taxonomy is the repo's full
treatment of exactly this indexing.
