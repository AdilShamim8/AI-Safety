## Core ideas, explained

### 1. Why institutions are a safety mechanism

The technical stack assumes an operator acting in good faith with
resources and mandate. Institutions supply the rest: evaluation
bodies with access (the AISI model), reporting regimes that make
incidents legible, standards that make safety claims comparable, and
coordination that prevents a race to the bottom. The repo's
[Ch. 23](../../chapters/23-governance-and-standards/README.md)
carries the governance machinery; the CAIS course's collective-action
lecture (in this library) teaches the underpinnings.

### 2. The measurement-commons problem

Institutions need shared instruments — common evals, incident
taxonomies, capability taxonomies — or safety claims fragment into
incomparable marketing. The repo's evaluation chapters exist partly
to make its readers literate *consumers* of exactly those instruments
when they arrive.

### 3. A lab teaching its own regulation

The self-interested reading (a lab shaping the rules it will live
under) and the sincere reading (the people closest to the risk
understanding what coordination requires) are both live; the repo's
method — price the source — applies. This course note carries that
pricing openly, as the register entry for the playlist does.
