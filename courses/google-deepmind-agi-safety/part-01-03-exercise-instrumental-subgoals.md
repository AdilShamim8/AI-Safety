## What this part covers

The course's first checkpoint exercise (2 minutes):
given goal descriptions, the learner identifies which instrumental
subgoals would serve each — self-preservation, resource acquisition,
capability improvement, goal-content integrity.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. Exercises as the compression format's integrity check

In a 90-minute course, the exercises are where understanding is
actually tested — and their placement (immediately after the
mechanism they test) is curriculum design as error correction. The
format exports well: the repo's chapter exercises and the Harvard
course's student experiments are the same pedagogy at different
depths.

### 2. The transferable skill: subgoal analysis

The skill being drilled — read a goal, derive its instrumental
structure, predict the behavior it pressures a capable optimizer
toward — is the day-one skill of threat modeling for agents. It is
exactly the move [Ch. 01](../../chapters/01-risk-models-and-research-epistemics/README.md)'s
threat-hypothesis exercise teaches and [Ch. 08](../../chapters/08-ai-control-and-monitoring/README.md)'s
control protocols assume you can do.

### 3. Worked-answer discipline

This library's own exercise treatment follows the repo's solutions
discipline: exercises have worked answers with stated reasoning
(see the repo's per-chapter solutions files) — a 2-minute quiz video
cannot carry that, so the note here points to the deeper drill
rather than paraphrasing the quiz.
