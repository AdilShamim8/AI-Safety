## Core ideas, explained

### 1. The RSP family as process, not document

Anthropic's RSP, OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, DeepMind's Frontier
Safety Framework — the course's own lab lives inside this family. The
substance is the loop: evaluate against named capability thresholds,
attach safeguards per tier, re-evaluate, publish enough for outsiders
to check the shape. The repo's
[Ch. 20](../../chapters/20-misuse-dangerous-capabilities-release-evaluation/README.md)
teaches the evaluation machinery; the Harvard lab-vs-field lecture
(this library) carries the externalist critique.

### 2. Safety cases as the bridging artifact

The safety-case discipline (structured arguments from evidence to
"risks below stated limits") is how lab practices become auditable:
claims decompose into sub-claims, each with evidence and stated
residual risk. The repo's [Ch. 22](../../chapters/22-safety-engineering-and-assurance/README.md)
and its assurance project are the full treatment of the same
artifact discipline.

### 3. The honest limits, stated by practitioners

Practitioners state the limits themselves: thresholds are uncertain,
evals are gameable, and the lab grading itself has an incentive
problem. The mitigation is external evaluation and transparency
minimums — which is why Part 3.1 (institutions) precedes this part:
the practices only anchor when someone outside can check them.
