## Where this lecture sits

The engineering-discipline lecture: how mature
high-consequence industries produce safety — safety cases, redundancy,
blameless incident culture, regulation — and what does and does not
transfer to AI.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. Safety as an engineered property, not a hope

The lecture imports the safety-engineering canon: hazards analysis,
defense in depth, fail-safe design, safety cases as structured
arguments, and the incident-reporting culture (aviation's
near-mandatory reporting, nuclear's INES scale) that turns failures
into institutional memory. The transferable core: safety comes from
*process plus evidence*, sustained by culture — never from
intentions.

### 2. What does not transfer

Software iterates weekly; bridges do not. AI's uniqueness argument:
rapid capability change under continuous deployment, statistical
(not deterministic) components, and distribution shift as a default
condition — the reasons you cannot simply certify-then-freeze. The
repo's [Ch. 22](../../chapters/22-safety-engineering-and-assurance/README.md)
makes the same argument with the assurance machinery attached.

### 3. The incident-database lesson

Aviation's safety record was built on mandatory, searchable,
blame-protected incident reporting — the model for the AI incident
databases this repo registers and the 14-incident record its notes
carry. The lecture's practical takeaway for any organization: your
incident taxonomy is your learning rate.
