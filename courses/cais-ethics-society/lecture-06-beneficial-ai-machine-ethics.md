## Where this lecture sits

The values lecture: beyond instruction-following —
what it would mean for AI to be *beneficial*; moral status and
machine ethics; whose values, plural, and how conflict among them is
handled.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. From obedient to beneficial

The lecture's first move problematizes the default: "do what I say"
is a bad target (orders are wrong, incomplete, or harmful), so the
field's real aspiration — assistance, deference to corrected
preferences, honesty even at cost — is a richer object than
compliance. The repo's preference-learning chapter
([Ch. 04](../../chapters/04-preference-learning-and-post-training/README.md))
is the technical face; this lecture is the philosophical face.

### 2. Moral uncertainty and pluralism

Whose values? The honest answer: many, conflicting, and the
conflict-resolution mechanism is itself a value choice. Constitutional
methods (the repo carries the Constitutional AI note) are the
engineering response — write the constitution, accept that it
encodes a drafter. The lecture keeps the political-philosophy stakes
visible: value learning is governance.

### 3. Machine ethics and moral status

The forward-looking block: if systems develop morally relevant
properties, welfare questions stop being hypothetical; and coding
ethical constraints into learning systems (top-down vs. bottom-up
ethics) has a long, mostly-humbling literature. The repo treats
welfare questions as open, priced accordingly — the Harvard course's
model-welfare guest lecture (Jeff Sebo, announced Fall 2026) is the
research-community signal that the topic is arriving.
