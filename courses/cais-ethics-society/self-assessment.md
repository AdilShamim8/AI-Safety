# AI Safety, Ethics & Society — the self-assessment

*Part of the CAIS course companion · 2026-10-27*

Ten questions on the eight-lecture arc, written for the
course's register: no mathematics, every question a judgment or a
distinction rather than a calculation.

## The instrument

**The risk landscape (1–3)**

1. Give the three-family risk taxonomy with one worked example each,
   and state why misclassifying a risk buys the wrong defense.
2. Name the four signature properties of ML systems as taught in the
   fundamentals lecture, and connect each to a risk family.
3. Why does the course title join "safety" and "ethics"? What shared
   question are the two communities answering?

**Single systems to systems of systems (4–7)**

4. What is the genie's-wish structure of specification gaming, and
   how does it differ from a side-effect failure?
5. Give the instrumental-convergence argument in civilian language:
   why does almost any goal pressure toward self-preservation?
6. What did mature high-consequence industries actually build — the
   four or five mechanisms — and which transfers least well to AI,
   and why?
7. What makes a risk "emergent between the parts"? Give one
   AI-era example of a feedback loop with an objective nobody chose.

**Society and governance (8–10)**

8. Why is corrigibility the hinge property between obedience and
   beneficial AI?
9. State the race argument: payoff structure, defection incentive,
   and one mechanism from analogous domains that has actually worked.
10. Organize the governance instruments (hard law, standards, soft
    commitments) by what each trades away, and say where the
    operational safety work actually happens.

**Pointers**: 1–3 — the lecture-01/02 notes; 4–7 — the lecture-03/04/05
notes; 8–10 — the lecture-06/07/08 notes.

## How to use this

Work each item in writing before looking at the pointers. The
questions are calibrated so that a learner who has only watched the
videos will find several of them sharp, and one who has also worked
the repository chapters will find them fair. That gap is the
diagnostic: it is what "watched" leaves out, measured.
