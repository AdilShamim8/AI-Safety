# How the CAIS course organizes knowledge — the pedagogy note

*Part of [AI Safety, Ethics, & Society](README.md) · written from the
verified course structure (2026-10-27)*

## The architecture, stated plainly

Eight lectures tracking a published textbook's arc, moving outward
in **circles of scope**: the individual system (fundamentals, single-
agent safety) → the engineered artifact (safety engineering) → the
system of systems (complex systems) → the society (ethics,
collective action, governance). Each circle introduces the failure
modes that only exist at that scale, assuming no technical
background anywhere.

## What the organization teaches

Three design claims. **Scope layering is the accessible substitute
for mathematical layering** — a non-technical audience cannot
decompose "the alignment problem" into components, but it can
decompose the *world* into scales, and the failure modes attach to
scales just as well. **A published textbook is the load-bearing
artifact** — the lectures exist to carry the book's arc to people
who watch but do not read; the book's stability (a fixed, citable
edition) anchors a course that videos alone could not. **Society-
scale topics are first-class safety content** — collective action,
structural risk, and governance get full lectures, not appendices;
the course's distinctive bet among this library's four.

## The information-gathering model

The course is a **synthesis artifact**: it gathers from the published
literature, law, and case history, then manages that material
through the textbook's editorial process (the only one of the four
courses with a formal publisher's review behind it). Its information
management is the book's index and citation apparatus — the
traditional technology for exactly this problem, and one this
repository deliberately re-creates digitally with its registers.

## What this repository adopted

The scope-circle arc (the repo's chapter ordering moves through the
same circles at technical depth), the commitment to teaching
collective action as core material (Ch. 21), and the fixed-citable-
edition principle for anything this library ships as a reference.
