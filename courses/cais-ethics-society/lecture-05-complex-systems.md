## Where this lecture sits

The systems lecture: when many agents — human and
AI — interact, new failure classes appear that no single system
exhibits: cascades, feedback loops, emergent coordination, systemic
fragility.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. Emergence as a risk category

The lecture's core move: interactions generate phenomena that
component-level analysis cannot see — flash-crash dynamics,
cascading defaults, ecosystem-level surveillance emerging from
individually-benign deployments. The repo's
[Ch. 21](../../chapters/21-sociotechnical-and-systemic-risk/README.md)
is this lecture's full treatment; the Harvard course's RSI and
economics lectures cover the growth-dynamics subset.

### 2. Feedback loops with AI in the loop

The distinctive modern pattern: models trained on data generated by
systems containing models — recommendation → behavior → training
data → better recommendation. Every such loop is an optimizer
wrapped around a human population, and the loop's objective (engagement)
is rarely what any participant chose. Model collapse and preference
homogenization are the measured versions of the worry.

### 3. Normal-accidents theory, imported

Perrow's tight-coupling/interactive-complexity frame transfers
directly: AI-rich systems are exactly the tightly-coupled,
interactively-complex class where normal accidents are expected, and
the design responses (loose coupling, buffering, slack) are the
countermeasures. The lecture pairs this with the collective-action
lecture (next in the arc) — system-level failures need
system-level governance.
