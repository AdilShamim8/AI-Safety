# Four courses, one field — where they agree

*Part of the [lecture library](../README.md) · 2026-10-27*

The four courses disagree about almost everything except the
following — and the overlap is the closest thing this library has to
a field consensus:

1. **The capability trend is real and does not flatten on its own.**
   Harvard prices it as measurement discipline; DeepMind states it as
   the course's first fact; CAIS teaches its societal reading;
   Safeshield treats it as the reason governance exists now rather
   than later.
2. **Specification is the central technical difficulty.** Every
   course teaches some version of the proxy-vs-goal problem — from
   Harvard's reading lists on reward hacking to Safeshield's risk
   framing of "intended purpose."
3. **Oversight must scale with capability.** Amplified oversight,
   control protocols, human-oversight design, audit practice — four
   vocabularies, one claim: supervision that does not scale is
   decoration.
4. **Institutions are load-bearing, not overhead.** RSPs, standards,
   evaluation bodies, audit regimes — every course ends at the same
   place: the technical stack assumes an institutional wrapper, and
   the wrapper is missing.
5. **Measurement is the discipline that holds it together.** Every
   course's method converges on the same move this repository makes
   canonical: convert worry into priced, evidenced, dated claims.

Where only one course teaches something, that something is a bet:
Harvard's student experiments, DeepMind's self-description, CAIS's
collective action, Safeshield's Monday-morning practice. The library
prices each accordingly.
