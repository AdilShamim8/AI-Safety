## Where this module sits

The second framework module: the NIST AI RMF — the
voluntary US framework whose four functions (Govern, Map, Measure,
Manage) have become the de facto vocabulary of AI risk practice,
with the Generative AI Profile as its modern extension.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. Four functions, one loop

**Govern** (roles, accountability, risk appetite — the culture
layer), **Map** (context: what the system is, for whom, in what
environment), **Measure** (evaluations: capabilities, robustness,
fairness, explainability — with the epistemics), **Manage**
(treatments, monitoring, incident response, decommissioning). The
structure's strength: Measure sits between Map and Manage, so
treatment is evidence-driven — the same architecture this repo's
Ch. 01 → Ch. 02 → applications arc enforces.

### 2. Voluntary-but-load-bearing

The RMF is voluntary, yet it anchors the US ecosystem: federal
agencies reference it, procurement demands it, and the EU AI Act's
harmonized-standard work borrowed its vocabulary. A framework
without legal force becoming infrastructure through adoption is
itself a governance lesson (the CAIS course's collective-action
lecture explains the mechanism).

### 3. The Generative AI Profile

The 2023 profile extends the RMF to foundation models: pretraining
data provenance, hallucination management, red-teaming, open-weight
considerations — the practitioner bridge from classic-ML risk to
the LLM era, and the natural companion to this repo's Ch. 04 and
Ch. 13–15 material.
