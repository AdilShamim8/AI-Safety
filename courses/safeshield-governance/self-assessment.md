# The AI Governance series — the self-assessment

*Part of the Safeshield Training companion · 2026-10-27*

Twelve practitioner questions: each one is the kind an
auditor, a regulator, or your own future incident review will actually
ask. Written answers only — this is the register the series teaches
in.

## The instrument

**Foundations (1–3)**

1. Name the four stakeholders of the AI governance map and one duty
   each holds. Which distinction does the EU AI Act's obligation
   split run along?
2. Take any two trustworthiness principles and state one real
   tension between them. What does mature governance do with that
   tension?
3. What is the responsibility gap, and what are the three closure
   mechanisms current law uses?

**Risk and frameworks (4–8)**

4. Write the risk-management cycle from memory, with the artifact
   each stage produces. Where does a risk appetite statement live,
   and what happens without one?
5. What does ISO/IEC 42001 certify — the model or the system? Give
   the two-word answer and its one-sentence justification.
6. Name the NIST AI RMF's four functions and explain why Measure
   sits between Map and Manage.
7. Draw the EU AI Act's risk pyramid with one example practice per
   tier. Where do GPAI obligations sit, and what triggers the
   systemic-risk tier?
8. What is the audit trail's time-travel query, and which five
   fields does any logged decision need?

**Operations (9–12)**

9. Which two lifecycle phases get the least attention and matter
   most in audits — and what does each require?
10. Write the policy pyramid's four layers, each answering which
    auditor question?
11. What makes an audit meaningful rather than ceremonial — the
    five elements?
12. What does the monitoring stack keep true after the auditors
    leave, and who must hold the authority to act on it?

**Pointers**: 1–3 — the module-01/02/03 notes; 4–8 — the module-08/09/10/11
notes; 9–12 — the module-12/13/14 notes.

## How to use this

Work each item in writing before looking at the pointers. The
questions are calibrated so that a learner who has only watched the
videos will find several of them sharp, and one who has also worked
the repository chapters will find them fair. That gap is the
diagnostic: it is what "watched" leaves out, measured.
