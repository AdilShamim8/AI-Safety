## Where this module sits

The series opener: what AI governance is, why it
emerged as a professional discipline, the vocabulary (risk, impact,
oversight, accountability), and the map of instruments the rest of
the series teaches.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. Governance as the missing third leg

The series' founding frame: capability and ethics got the attention;
governance is what makes either binding. An organization can have
principles without processes, models without owners, and impact
without accountability — governance is the discipline that closes
those gaps. The professional's map: principles (why) → risk
management (how) → frameworks and law (what is required) → lifecycle
operations (when and who).

### 2. The stakeholder map

Foundations means knowing who holds which duty: the provider
(builds the system), the deployer (puts it to work), the affected
party (bears the impact), and the regulator (enforces the floor).
Modern AI law — especially the EU AI Act's obligation split — is
unreadable without this map, and the repo's
[Ch. 23](../../chapters/23-governance-and-standards/README.md)
organizes its treatment the same way.

### 3. Why practitioners, not only policymakers

The series teaches governance as an operational skill: someone in
every organization must be able to inventory AI systems, classify
risk, document decisions, and survive an audit. That is the Monday-
morning layer this library's other three courses do not attempt.
