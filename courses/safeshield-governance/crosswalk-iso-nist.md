# Crosswalk — ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF

*Part of the [Safeshield governance companion](README.md) · 2026-10-27*

Two instruments dominate AI governance practice: a certifiable
management-system standard (ISO) and a voluntary risk framework
(NIST). They are complements, not competitors — and the fastest way
to see it is the crosswalk.

| Dimension | ISO/IEC 42001 | NIST AI RMF |
|---|---|---|
| Legal character | Certifiable standard (third-party audit; procurement carries it) | Voluntary framework (adoption-driven authority) |
| Origin lineage | ISO 9001/27001 management-system family | US federal risk practice (NIST's cybersecurity lineage) |
| Core structure | Plan-Do-Check-Act over an AI management system (AIMS) | Govern–Map–Measure–Manage functions |
| Unit of governance | The organization's AI processes | The AI system's risk lifecycle |
| Evidence artifact | Management reviews, corrective actions, audit records | Risk registers, measurement plans, profiles |
| Enforcement teeth | Certification, contracts, procurement | Agency expectations, market norms |
| Update cadence | Standard revision cycles (slow, stable) | Profiles and generations (faster; the GenAI Profile, 2023) |

**Where they interlock.** The RMF's Govern function supplies the
organizational machinery an AIMS audit inspects; ISO's certification
gives the RMF's voluntary practice a market signal. Organizations
running both typically let the RMF structure the risk work and let
42001's audit discipline prove it runs. The repo's
[Ch. 23](../../chapters/23-governance-and-standards/README.md)
carries both at concept depth; this crosswalk is the practitioner's
one-page version.
