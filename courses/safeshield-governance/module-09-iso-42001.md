## Core ideas, explained

### 1. What a management-system standard actually is

ISO 42001 is not a technical standard — it is an organizational
one, in the lineage of ISO 9001 (quality) and ISO 27001 (information
security): define scope, assign roles, run documented processes,
measure them, review them, improve them — this time for AI. The
certifiable object is the *system*, not the model: an organization
can hold a certificate while running imperfect models, if its
processes for finding and correcting imperfection are real.

### 2. The AI-specific controls

What distinguishes 42001 from its ancestors: AI impact assessments
for intended use, data governance controls across the lifecycle,
controls on supplier and asset management for AI systems, and
documented roles (the "AI management representative"). The
Harvard course's model-spec lecture (in this library) meets this
world here: the spec is the policy; 42001 is the machinery that
makes policy operational.

### 3. Audit reality

The module's practitioner content: what a certification audit
actually inspects (documented processes, records of operation,
management review, corrective actions), and the standard critique —
management systems can bureaucratize without improving, and a
certificate is evidence of process, not of safety. The repo's
honesty rules are the antidote at learner scale: the maturity
values in its own planning matrix must match reality on disk.
