## Where this module sits

The integrating module: risk management as the
discipline that converts principles into decisions — identify,
analyze, evaluate, treat, monitor — with AI-specific hazards in every
stage.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. The classical cycle, AI-loaded

ISO 31000's loop (context → identification → analysis → evaluation →
treatment → monitoring) with AI's specific hazard inventory at each
stage: capability hazards, misuse pathways, sociotechnical
interactions, and the measurement problem that haunts evaluation —
you cannot quantify what your evaluation cannot construct-validly
measure. The module's practical core is the risk register: every
entry with an owner, a treatment, a residual, and a review date.

### 2. Risk appetite as the governance decision

The step most organizations skip: stating, in advance and in
writing, how much of which risks the organization accepts. Without
a stated appetite, every incident becomes an improvised referendum;
with one, the incident becomes a comparison. This repo's method —
safety as a claim with stated limits — is the research-scale version
of the same discipline (see
[Ch. 01](../../chapters/01-risk-models-and-research-epistemics/README.md)
and [Ch. 22](../../chapters/22-safety-engineering-and-assurance/README.md)).

### 3. The frameworks converge here

NIST AI RMF (module 10) and ISO 42001 (module 9) are both, at their
cores, risk-management cycles with different vocabularies and
different enforcement teeth. Learning the cycle once makes both
frameworks legible — which is why this module sits between the
principles band and the frameworks band in the series.
