## What this course is

This is Safeshield Training's AI governance series — practitioner training,
not research pedagogy. Where the other three courses in this library ask
"what could go wrong and who is studying it," this one asks the operator's
question: **what does a compliant, auditable AI management system look like
on Monday morning?** Its subject matter is the machinery of governance —
principles, risk management processes, management-system standards, and
audit — taught at the depth a working GRC (governance, risk, compliance)
professional needs.

That makes it the fourth pillar of this library by design: the repo's
Chapters 22–23 teach safety engineering and governance *conceptually*, and
this series is the practitioner's counterpart — the same frameworks
(ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act) taught as implementation
and audit practice. Fourteen modules, roughly 40–60 minutes each: a full
professional curriculum in about 12.5 hours.

## The three bands of the series

- **Band 1 — principles (modules 1–2).** What governance is for: foundations
  and the principle layer (accountability, transparency, fairness, privacy,
  safety, human oversight).
- **Band 2 — the principle modules (modules 3–8).** One video per governance
  principle as it lands in practice: accountability and traceability;
  transparency and explainability; fairness and non-discrimination; privacy
  and data protection; safety, security, and resilience; and risk management
  as the integrating discipline.
- **Band 3 — the frameworks and the lifecycle (modules 9–14).** The named
  instruments practitioners actually implement and audit against: ISO/IEC
  42001 (the AI management-system standard), the NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act,
  then the operational layer — AI system lifecycle governance, policies and
  documentation, and monitoring, auditing, and compliance.

## How the course organizes knowledge

The series teaches in **framework layers**: principles first (why), then
risk management as the bridge (how, in the abstract), then the named
frameworks (how, in enforceable detail), then the operational lifecycle
(when, and who). It is the only course in this library where the reading
matter is law and standards rather than papers — and its register treatment
here reflects that: the frameworks it teaches (ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF,
EU AI Act) are the primary sources, with the series as the accessible
guided tour of them.
