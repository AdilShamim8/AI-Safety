## Where this module sits

The principles module: the trustworthiness cluster —
accountability, transparency, fairness, privacy, safety, human
oversight — each defined and each traced to its origin in soft law
(OECD, UNESCO) and hard law (the EU AI Act's Article bases).

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. Principles as legalized vocabulary

The module's quiet insight: what began as aspirational AI-ethics
lists (OECD AI Principles, UNESCO's recommendation) has hardened
into operational law — the EU AI Act's transparency and human-
oversight obligations, fairness in anti-discrimination law, privacy
in GDPR-class statutes. A principle without an enforcement pathway
is decoration; the module maps each principle to both its soft origin
and its hard landing.

### 2. Human oversight as a designed mechanism

The principle with the most engineering content: meaningful human
oversight is not a signature on a form — it is the design of the
human's information, time, authority, and ability to refuse. The
over-automation literature (and every aviation auto-flight accident)
shows what fails when "human in the loop" is a staffing decision
instead of a design one. The repo treats oversight as an engineered
protocol in [Ch. 08](../../chapters/08-ai-control-and-monitoring/README.md).

### 3. The principles' internal tensions, priced

Fairness metrics conflict (calibration vs. equalized odds); transparency
conflicts with IP and security; safety conflicts with availability.
Mature governance does not resolve these tensions — it documents the
trade-off decision, who made it, and on what evidence. That
documentation habit is exactly this repo's register discipline,
translated to compliance.
