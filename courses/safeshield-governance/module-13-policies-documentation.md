## Where this module sits

The documentation module: the policy hierarchy —
from board-level AI policy through procedures to work instructions —
and the artifact set (impact assessments, inventories, evaluation
records) that makes governance inspectable.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. The policy pyramid

Board policy (risk appetite, red lines) → governance procedures
(the risk process, the gate criteria) → work instructions (how this
team runs this evaluation) → records (the evidence the above
produced). Each layer answers a different auditor question, and the
module's craft content is writing each layer at the right altitude —
policies vague enough to survive change, instructions concrete
enough to execute.

### 2. The inventory as the first artifact

You cannot govern what you have not listed: the AI system inventory
(system, owner, purpose, risk class, lifecycle stage, dependencies)
is the first artifact every framework demands — and the one whose
absence every incident investigation discovers too late. The repo's
own inventory discipline (its content matrix and registers) is the
same artifact at repository scale.

### 3. Documentation as designed for audits

The practitioner's craft: write every document for the reader who
arrives hostile, uninformed, and in a hurry — the auditor, the
regulator, or your successor after the incident. This repository's
entire method — registers with access levels, honest limitations,
dated evidence — is that writing discipline, practiced.
