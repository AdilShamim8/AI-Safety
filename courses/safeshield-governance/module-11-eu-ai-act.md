## Where this module sits

The third framework module: the EU AI Act — the
first comprehensive horizontal AI statute: the risk pyramid, the
obligation split, the timeline, and what "conformity" actually
demands.

## Core ideas, explained

### 1. The risk pyramid as legal architecture

Unacceptable risk (prohibited practices — social scoring,
manipulative techniques, untargeted biometric scraping), high risk
(the conformity-assessment regime: documentation, data governance,
logging, human oversight, robustness), limited risk (transparency
duties — the chatbot-disclosure class), minimal risk (no specific
duties). The pyramid's legal power: obligations attach to *intended
purpose and use context*, not to technology category — a governance
decision with real edge cases (is your tool high-risk or not?) that
the module's practitioner content works through.

### 2. GPAI obligations and the frontier tier

The Act's general-purpose AI chapter: documentation and
transparency duties for all GPAI providers, plus the systemic-risk
tier (training-compute threshold plus the EU's assessment powers)
with evaluation, adversarial-testing, and incident-reporting duties.
This is the statute meeting the frontier-lab practices the Harvard
course's lab-vs-field lecture surveys — the repo's
[Ch. 20](../../chapters/20-misuse-dangerous-capabilities-release-evaluation/README.md)
teaches the evaluation machinery the duties presume.

### 3. Timeline and enforcement reality

Entry into force 2024 with staged application (prohibitions and AI
literacy in 2025, GPAI duties in 2025, most high-risk duties in
2026–2027) and member-state enforcement authorities still
standing up. The module's honest frame: the Act is the world's
largest AI governance experiment in progress, not a settled rulebook
— the repo's register prices the timeline page it maintains
accordingly.
