# How CS 2881 organizes knowledge — the pedagogy note

*Part of [Harvard CS 2881 — AI Safety](README.md) · written from the
verified course structure (2026-10-27)*

## The architecture, stated plainly

CS 2881 is a **weekly research seminar with an evidence discipline**.
Each of its thirteen sessions is one topic, assembled from four
load-bearing parts, in this order:

1. **Required pre-readings, discussed before the lecture.** Primary
   sources only — incident reports, technical reports, preprints —
   read on Perusall, where substantive annotation is part of the
   grade. The discussion happens *before* the guest arrives, so the
   room is prepared to interrogate rather than receive.
2. **A guest who works on the topic.** Practitioners and researchers
   (Carlini on security, Shlegeris and Hobbhahn on scheming, METR on
   measurement, Lambert on open models, Toner on regulation). The
   course treats working contact with the field's actual people as
   curricular content, not enrichment.
3. **A student experiment, presented live.** Every lecture closes
   with a bounded empirical study a student group ran that week —
   fine-tuning studies, jailbreak red-teams, pressure experiments —
   presented with failures included. The experiments are graded as
   research communication, and the course's own experiment videos
   are published beside the lectures.
4. **Scribe notes and a final project.** Students write the record
   and defend a capstone — the research-apprenticeship loop closing.

## What the organization teaches

The design encodes three claims. **Safety knowledge is built like
other empirical knowledge** — read primary sources, run the small
study, present the result including its failures; the course's
structure is a claim that this is *the* method, not one method. **The
field's unit of progress is the working practitioner** — hence
guests over textbook chapters, and experiments over problem sets.
**Measurement and scale are the two axes** — the introduction's own
framing, visible in which readings recur (METR's measurements in
lectures 1, 7, and 12; scaling considerations in nearly every
technical lecture).

## The information-gathering model

The course gathers information the way a lab does: scheduled primary
sources (the reading lists, reproduced in this library's notes),
direct testimony (the guests), and original generation (the
experiments). It manages the record through scribe notes, published
lecture materials, and — its most exportable practice — **published
student work**: the experiments' code repositories and write-ups are
linked from the course page, making the seminar's output a public
artifact rather than a private grade.

## What this repository adopted

The reading-list-as-spine (register discipline), the
experiment-with-failures-included presentation style (this repo's
project reports and their honest not-executed tiers), and the
defended-capstone assessment (project P10). See
[how-courses-organize.md](../how-courses-organize.md) for the
cross-course synthesis.
